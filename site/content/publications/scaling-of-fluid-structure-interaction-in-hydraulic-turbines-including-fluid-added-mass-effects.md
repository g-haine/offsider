## Abstract
Hydraulic turbines are subjected to elevated dynamic fluid loads as a result of growing flexibility requirements. Therefore, designs that account for flow-induced vibrations have become essential for structural integrity. Reduced-scale experiments are increasingly used to assess dynamic structural behavior. However, scaling near-resonant vibrations from model to prototype remains challenging for submerged structures. In particular, uncertainties persist regarding the validity of classical similarity laws for predicting submerged modal behavior. To address these challenges, the eigenfrequencies and mode shapes of a high-head pump-turbine are investigated numerically in this study at both prototype and model scales using coupled acoustic-structural modal analysis. To enable flexible material choice in model tests, three different structural materials are considered for the model-scale runner. While good agreement between model and prototype is observed in vacuum, application of conventional scaling laws in water leads to systematic discrepancies in eigenfrequencies for differing model and prototype materials. We reveal that these deviations arise from the dominant added-mass effect, which is absent in the classical scaling approach. To address this limitation, a novel scaling framework explicitly incorporating the governing physics of fluid-added mass is derived. As a result, the proposed scaling accurately predicts submerged eigenfrequencies, eliminating discrepancies across all tested materials. Finally, the implications of the scaling framework for defining model-scale testing conditions are discussed. The proposed approach enables reliable model-scale testing with prototype-representative modal behavior in hydraulic turbines and is expected to be applicable to other submerged structures governed by similar fluid-structure interaction mechanisms.
