## Abstract
Moving mass control (MMC) is a deformation control method in problems involving fluid–solid interaction (FSI). However, there is a lack of governing equations' deduction and control methods in the current MMC literature of nonlinear FSI systems. In this research, the flow-induced vibration of a flexible beam attached to a rigid cylinder in a downstream flow is studied. The interaction between solid and concentrated mass is derived based on Hamilton's principle with consideration of the nonlinearity of the problem. With gravity introduced, a new MMC control law of concentrated mass location is proposed where the target point is searched by maximizing the negative work of the moving mass. Then, the concentrated mass is forced to chase the target point by proportion differentiation control law. Meanwhile, the moving concentrated mass is restricted by a velocity limiter. The structural vibration amplitude turns out to be suppressed by 83.5% via the new MMC method. A similar suppression is also found in fluid oscillation. A mode analysis shows that the flow structure that contributes to the beam vibration is weakened. According to comparative analysis, the velocity limit turns out to be important in chasing up the target point while reducing the negative influence of dynamic terms. Furthermore, the performance of MMC in a time-varying flow is then investigated, where high adaptiveness in different inlet flows can also be seen in the proposed approach.
